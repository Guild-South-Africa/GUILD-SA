04 / Guild Sprint / Hackathon / Demo Day

Every minute has a purpose.

A GUILD SA event is not a casual meetup. It is a structured production environment designed around pressure, collaboration, execution, visibility, and shipped outcomes.

Every phase is intentional. Every constraint exists to force clarity.

01

Event day structure

The schedule protects the build.

The format is engineered for momentum. Planning is focused. Build blocks are protected. Checkpoints reduce drift. Demos happen whether teams feel ready or not. The environment rewards execution over perfection.

07:30

Setup

Prepare the environment. Venue staging, technical setup, registration systems, branding, QR infrastructure, badges, lanyards, and workspace readiness. The space must feel operational before the first builder arrives.

08:30

Opening ceremony

Align the room. Mission, rules, sprint structure, judging logic, and expectations are introduced. Core principle declared: No MVP = No Judgement.

09:00

Planning phase

Define the execution path. Teams clarify the problem, MVP scope, technical stack, roles, and delivery approach. Strong planning reduces weak execution.

10:00

Build block 01

Enter focus mode. Teams move into uninterrupted execution. Mentors circulate only when needed. Scope discipline becomes critical. Momentum begins compounding.

12:00

Checkpoint 01

Surface risk early. Every team delivers a concise progress update. Mentors identify scope inflation, technical blockers, weak problem framing, and delivery risk.

13:00

Build block 02

Push toward deployment. The environment intensifies. Commits increase. Scope narrows. Decisions accelerate. The goal is no longer an idea. The goal is a shippable product.

16:30

Submission deadline

Lock the build. Teams submit deployed links, GitHub repositories, demo access, and required assets. No late submissions. No unfinished concepts. No invisible work.

17:00

Demo session

Prove the work publicly. Teams receive 3-5 minute presentation windows, live product walkthroughs, technical questioning, public evaluation, and QR-based audience voting.

19:30

Results & awards

Transition into continuation. Top teams are recognised publicly. Guild Labs pathways are introduced. Industry conversations begin. The awards are not the finish line. They are the signal.

100

Judging system

Teams are evaluated on output, relevance, and execution quality.

The scorecard prioritises functional delivery, problem clarity, South African relevance, innovation, and meaningful technical execution.

The system rewards teams that build usable products under real constraints.

25%

MVP execution

Does the product work? Can the team demonstrate a functioning MVP live and explain its implementation clearly?

30%

Impact potential

Does the solution matter? Could the project scale or solve a meaningful South African problem?

25%

Problem fit

Is the solution aligned? Does the MVP genuinely address the stated problem with clarity and usability?

20%

Innovation

Is there meaningful edge? Creative thinking, technical depth, AI integration, and differentiated execution are rewarded.

The awards are not the finish line.

They are the signal.

72h

Post-event system

The sprint continues after the venue closes.

The strongest signal exists immediately after the event. Project evidence, momentum, and visibility are captured while attention is still active.

The ecosystem moves quickly to convert projects into proof, demos into visibility, and participation into opportunity.

Capture

Preserve the evidence.

The system documents GitHub links, screenshots, demo footage, project summaries, team photos, technical stacks, and participant insights.

Publish

Push the signal outward.

Projects become showcase pages, recap reels, sponsor-ready reports, social assets, and public case studies. Momentum becomes visible.

Follow-up

Continue the strongest work.

Selected teams receive Labs invitations, mentorship continuation, partner visibility, ecosystem onboarding, and structured follow-up.

Build under pressure. Ship in public.

GUILD SA events simulate the intensity, urgency, and visibility of real product environments. The goal is not comfort. The goal is execution.
